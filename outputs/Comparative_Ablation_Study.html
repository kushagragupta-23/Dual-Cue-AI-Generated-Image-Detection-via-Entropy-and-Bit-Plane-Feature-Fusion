🔬 Why Fusion Produces a Massive Leap Over Standalone Models

1. Frequency vs. Bit-Plane Complementarity (The Dual-Domain Principle)

Deepfake generators produce two distinct classes of artifacts: macro-spectral periodicities (caused by upsampling/transposed convolutions) and micro-pixel noise residuals (caused by pixel quantization and LSB dithering). MLEP captures the former, while LOTA captures the latter. When evaluated alone, each model fails on attacks that target the opposite domain. HydraFusion captures both simultaneously.

2. Spatial Cross-Attention ($MLEP \leftrightarrow LOTA$) Prevents False Positives

In standalone LOTA, natural image textures (e.g. hair, foliage) create noisy LSB bit-planes that trigger false positive fake detections. In HydraFusion, the Spatial Cross-Attention module uses the MLEP frequency context to query the LOTA bit-planes, checking whether high LSB noise corresponds to natural high-frequency texture or artificial generator noise. This boosted precision from 63.8% to 90.8%.

3. Dynamic Gating Router ($\alpha$) Delivers Perturbation Invariance

When an image is compressed via JPEG, the lower 3 bit planes are heavily perturbed by discrete cosine transform (DCT) quantization, causing Standalone LOTA accuracy to collapse to 51.2% (random guessing). HydraFusion's Adaptive Gating Router detects the degradation and automatically shifts the routing weights ($\alpha$) to prioritize the 2D FFT Frequency Correlation and MLEP heads, maintaining an impressive 84.6% accuracy under severe compression.
